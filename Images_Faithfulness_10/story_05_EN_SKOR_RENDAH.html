## KG Context (What are the key facts and timeline of the 2022 Hormozgan earthquakes?): The 2022 Hormozgan earthquakes were a series of doublet earthquakes that occurred in southern Iran on July 1, 2022. These earthquakes resulted in seven fatalities and injured dozens more [1]. The tectonic setting for these earthquakes is the southern margin of the collision zone between the Eurasian Plate and the Arabian Plate, which formed the Zagros Mountains and the Iranian Plateau. The Zagros fold and thrust belt is the primary fault system responsible for seismic activity in this region [1]. The earthquake sequence began with a magnitude 6.0 event, followed by a magnitude 5.7 aftershock approximately two hours later. A subsequent magnitude 6.0 earthquake occurred one minute after the 5.7 aftershock. While initially reported with slightly higher magnitudes, these were later revised downwards [1]. By July 3, there had been twelve aftershocks, with the strongest measuring 5.7 Mw. Further aftershocks on July 23 measured 5.4 and 5.6 Mw, causing additional damage to houses and one indirect injury [1]. The maximum intensity of the earthquakes was recorded as VII (Very strong), with the most intense shaking reported in the Hormozgan and Fars provinces. The tremors were felt across the Middle East, including in the United Arab Emirates, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, and Qatar, as well as in parts of Pakistan and Afghanistan [1]. The impact of the earthquakes affected twelve towns and over 300 villages, impacting a population of approximately 900,000. The village of Sayeh Khvosh, with about 1,100 inhabitants, was completely destroyed. Rebuilding efforts for Sayeh Khvosh were estimated to take several months. In Bandar Khamir, at least 45 houses were affected, and 35 houses were damaged in the town of Kong. In total, at least 392 houses were damaged or destroyed. The earthquakes also caused power outages, and a landslide blocked the road between Bandar Khamir and Bandar Lengeh [1]. ### References * [1] 2022 Hormozgan Earthquakes
